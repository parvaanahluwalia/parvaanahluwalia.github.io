01 The brief

F1 in Schools, now called STEM Racing, is a competition where students design, build and race miniature CO₂-powered cars. Every car has to pass strict dimensional rules.

The starting point was the car as an STL file: a mesh of triangles. That’s fine for 3D printing, but it can’t be measured or edited reliably like a real CAD model. The job was to turn it into a proper STEP solid that could be checked against the rules.

02 What I did

  1. Converted the car’s STL mesh into a STEP solid using CadQuery and OpenCASCADE.
  2. Removed the embossed text from the body.
  3. Rebuilt the NURBS surfaces so the body is smooth, continuous geometry.
  4. Verified dimensional compliance against the regulations.
  5. Analysed the hub geometry and selected 3 × 6 × 2.5 mm bearings, comparing hybrid and full-ceramic options.

03 Engineering notes

Mesh vs solid

An STL describes a surface as thousands of flat triangles. A STEP file describes true solid geometry, so CAD tools can measure it precisely, which is exactly what a compliance check needs.

CAD as code

CadQuery is Python on top of the OpenCASCADE kernel, so the conversion is a script rather than a series of clicks. It can be re-run and adjusted.

Reading a bearing size

3 × 6 × 2.5 mm means a 3 mm bore, 6 mm outer diameter and 2.5 mm width. Hybrid bearings use ceramic balls in steel races; full-ceramic bearings use ceramic throughout.
